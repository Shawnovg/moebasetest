2 · The data

The task: the relation between two clauses

<cls> ( a ( and b ) ) #SEP# a

label < : every world where A holds, B holds

Bowman et al. (2015) generator, Ordered Memory release: 249,069 pairs over 6 variables. Train on ≤ 6 operators; test on held-out ≤ 6 and on unseen 7–12.

Each example is two clauses built from the six variables a to f with and, or and not. A clause's truth set is the set of the 64 possible worlds in which it is true. The label is the set relation between the two truth sets: forward or reverse entailment, equivalence, negation (complement), alternation (disjoint, not covering), cover (overlapping and covering) or independence. Because the generator rejects tautologies and contradictions, the label is a fixed function of four region bits: do A and B overlap, does A have worlds outside B, B outside A, and is there a world where neither holds. That is the structure the probes later look for. The input format is the same for every model: a CLS token, clause A, a separator, clause B, with the classifier reading the CLS state. Splits (length regime): train 121,977 and valid 13,552 pairs with at most 6 operators per clause; test 23,922 held-out pairs of the same lengths and 13,445 pairs with 7 to 12 operators, never seen in training. Independence is 54% of the data, so always guessing it gives 0.54. Equivalence and negation are rare (about 2% each).
